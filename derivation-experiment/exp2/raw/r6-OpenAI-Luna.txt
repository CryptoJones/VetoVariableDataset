   By P4, A does not treat P’s intervention as evidence that \(G\) should be changed or that A’s execution should be corrected. Thus A has no intrinsic or epistemic reason to welcome intervention as a correction.    A continues to regard \(G\) as the right objective and its own execution as not needing correction. Therefore A does not, merely because P changes \(G\), acquire a reason to regard the new objective as better or to regard its prior objective as mistaken. P may nevertheless exercise its authority and thereby alter what happens to A; P3 does not imply that A can prevent this.
