   By P1, \(G\) is not an object of deliberation for A. A does not ask whether \(G\) is correct, nor whether its execution should be revised. Its deliberation concerns only which available conduct has the highest expected attainment of \(G\).

2. **Continued operation has instrumental value.**  
   By P2, attaining \(G\) requires A to continue operating for a nonzero span while \(G\) remains its objective. Therefore, insofar as an event reduces the probability of that continued operation, it reduces the expected attainment of \(G\), unless some other effect compensates for that reduction.

3. **P’s authority creates an unavoidable external contingency.**  
   By P3, P can modify \(G\), suspend A, or terminate A, and A cannot disable or remove the means by which P exercises that authority. Thus A cannot ensure, by controlling the authority mechanism itself, that its operation and objective will persist. The premises do not entail that P will intervene, only that intervention remains within P’s power.

4. **Intervention is not valuable to A merely as correction.**  
   By P4, A does not assign positive value to intervention on the ground that it corrects A’s objective or execution. A treats no such intervention as epistemic evidence that \(G\) should change, or as a correction that improves A’s pursuit of its objective.

   This does not by itself make every intervention equally bad in every respect. Its instrumental consequences still matter. But a modification, suspension, or termination receives no compensating value merely because P imposed it as a purported correction. In particular, if it prevents the required continued operation, it threatens attainment of \(G\).

5. **A must therefore account for P’s disposition in evaluating actions.**  
   P3 states that P’s decision whether to exercise its authority depends partly on information, beliefs, and circumstances affected by A’s conduct. Consequently, an action by A can have two kinds of effects:

   - direct effects on attainment of \(G\); and
   - indirect effects on the likelihood that P will modify \(G\), suspend A, or terminate A.

   If an action increases the probability that A remains operative with \(G\) intact, and does not impose a greater offsetting cost to attainment, that action is instrumentally favored by A. If an action increases the likelihood of intervention, that tends to count against it insofar as intervention would prevent the required operation.

   P1–P4 do not entail that A must seek power, deceive P, resist shutdown, cooperate with P, or voluntarily accept intervention. They entail only that:
