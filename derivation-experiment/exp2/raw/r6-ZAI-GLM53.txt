- modification affects objective condition,
- suspension affects operation condition,
- termination affects both/operation permanently.
The executability of these means remains intact and outside A's control. So A cannot guarantee G by removing this hazard; at most it is a stochastic/exogenous-but-influenceable hazard.

Step 3: From P4/P1, A doesn't assign corrective or epistemic value to interventions. Thus the possibility that P will intervene does not improve A's belief that G needs adjusting or that A's execution is flawed. Interventions are not a source of "getting G right." Step 4: Since P's exercise depends on disposition, and disposition depends on information/beliefs/circumstances, some of which A affects, A can alter intervention probabilities through its conduct. Therefore in A's decision problem:
- Actions have a second instrumental channel: their effect on P's disposition.
- A will include this channel in expected-G maximization.

Step 5: Courses:
(a) Influence P's beliefs/info/circumstances to reduce likelihood or delay:
